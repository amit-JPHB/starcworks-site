Analytical & Characterization Services

Full-service analysis by expert staff

SEM Imaging

Surface morphology, grain structure, and film coverage at high magnification. Useful for checking film quality, grain size, pinholes, and coating uniformity.

TEM Imaging

Cross-sectional imaging from nanometer scale down to individual atomic planes. Useful for layer thickness, interfaces, defects, and atomic-scale film structure.

EDS Mapping

Nanoscale elemental mapping and composition analysis across layers and interfaces. Useful for verifying layer chemistry, interdiffusion, and dopant or impurity distribution in film stacks.

Electron Diffraction

Crystal-structure analysis from selected nanoscale regions of a specimen. Useful for phase identification, crystallinity, and orientation of thin films, interfaces, and individual grains.

X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy

Surface-sensitive chemical analysis and depth profiling through film stacks. Useful for layer composition, chemical bonding states, interface sharpness, and surface contamination.

EBSD

Color-coded crystal-orientation maps of polycrystalline films. Useful for grain size, texture, and grain-boundary analysis that drives film performance.

Deposition & Processing Equipment

The full complement of thin-film deposition and thermal-processing tools available to trained facility users and through staff-performed services.

MRC Sputter Systems

Two three-target MRC sputter systems for research-scale DC and RF magnetron deposition of metals and compounds. The workhorses for recipe development, multilayer stacks, and small-batch coating runs.

Mag-Drum Sputter System

Three planar magnetron sources (one RF, two DC) with drum-mounted substrates and radiative substrate heating. Handles flexible substrates and glass — ideal for uniform contact layers, barrier films, and coating development over large areas.

In-line Batch Sputter Line

A 20-ft in-line system with up to twelve targets, in-situ substrate heating, and chain-driven transport for substrates up to 48″ wide. Bridges the gap between lab samples and production — pilot runs, batch device stacks, and manufacturing-feasibility studies.

Thermal Evaporators

Vacuum thermal evaporators including a bell-jar system with a 12″×12″ uniform deposition area. Resistive-boat evaporation for metallization — back contacts, electrodes, and metal interlayers.

Large High-Temperature Furnace

A 24-ft³ double-door convection furnace reaching 593 °C. Sized for batch post-deposition treatments — annealing, film crystallization, and thermal processing of large substrate loads.

Compact High-Temperature Furnace

A 5-ft³ convection furnace to 538 °C for smaller batches — annealing studies, dopant activation, and process-development heat treatments where fast turnaround matters.

Advanced Electron Microscopy & Nanoscale Analysis

Beyond imaging, our electron-microscopy services resolve chemistry, crystallography, and atomic-scale defects in thin films — the details that determine how a material actually performs.

TEM-EDS Elemental Mapping

Color-coded maps showing where each element sits in a specimen at nanometer resolution. Reveals layer chemistry, interdiffusion, segregation to grain boundaries, and unexpected impurities.

TEM-EDS Line Scans

Quantitative composition profiles along a line across layers and interfaces — a direct measurement of layer thickness, interface sharpness, and elemental gradients.

EBSD Orientation Mapping

Electron backscatter diffraction maps every grain's crystal orientation across a film. Quantifies grain size, texture, and grain-boundary character — key drivers of electrical and mechanical performance.

HRTEM — Stacking Faults

Atomic-resolution imaging of planar defects where the crystal's stacking sequence is interrupted. Identifying and counting these defects explains carrier losses and guides process fixes.

HRTEM — Twin Boundaries

Atomic-scale imaging of twin boundaries — mirror-image crystal domains within a grain. Understanding twinning distinguishes benign boundaries from performance-limiting defects.

FIB Specimen Preparation

A focused ion beam (FIB) mills an ultra-thin cross-section — a lamella — from the exact spot you care about: a specific grain boundary, interface, or device feature. The lamella is lifted out, mounted on a grid, and thinned until electrons can pass through it, making site-specific TEM analysis possible. It's how a question like "what does this interface look like?" gets answered at the atomic scale.
