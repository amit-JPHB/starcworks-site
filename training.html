Thin Films Certification

6 weeks · ~48 hours · lecture + hands-on lab

A hands-on introduction to how thin films are made, measured, and used. Trainees run real deposition equipment, build multilayer film stacks, and learn to read what a film is telling them — the core skill set of the coating and semiconductor industries.

Topics covered

  • Thin-film fundamentals — nucleation, growth modes, and microstructure
  • Sputter deposition — DC and RF magnetron sources, targets, and plasmas
  • Thermal evaporation — sources, boats, and metallization practice
  • Process parameters — pressure, power, temperature, and gas flow, and how each shapes the film
  • Substrate preparation, cleaning, and adhesion
  • Film thickness and uniformity metrology
  • Film stress, defects, and common failure modes
  • Post-deposition treatments — annealing and crystallization
  • Characterization basics — microscopy, profilometry, and optical inspection
  • Capstone lab — design, deposit, and qualify a multilayer film stack

Semiconductors Certification

8 weeks · ~64 hours · lecture + hands-on lab

The materials, devices, and manufacturing processes behind the modern semiconductor industry — taught for technicians and career-changers, not theorists. Trainees leave able to speak the language of a fab and handle wafers and processes with confidence.

Topics covered

  • Semiconductor materials — silicon, compound semiconductors, and why band gaps matter
  • Doping, junctions, and how devices switch
  • Device overview — diodes, transistors, sensors, and photovoltaics
  • Wafer handling, contamination control, and cleanroom protocol
  • The process flow — deposition, lithography and patterning, etch, and thermal steps
  • Thin-film deposition for devices — hands-on on STARC equipment
  • Electrical testing — I–V and C–V measurement, four-point probe
  • Metrology, defects, and yield — finding and fixing what went wrong
  • The semiconductor industry landscape — roles, supply chain, and career paths
  • Capstone — build and electrically qualify a simple device structure

Vacuum Systems Certification

6 weeks · ~48 hours · lecture + hands-on lab

Vacuum technology underpins every deposition tool, and people who can design, operate, and troubleshoot vacuum systems are rare and in demand. This program covers the full stack — from gas physics to leak hunting on real hardware.

Topics covered

  • Vacuum fundamentals — pressure ranges, gas laws, and mean free path
  • Roughing pumps — rotary vane, scroll, and dry pumps
  • High-vacuum pumps — turbomolecular, diffusion, and cryo pumps
  • Pressure measurement — Pirani, capacitance manometer, and ion gauges
  • Hardware — flanges and seals (KF, ISO, CF), valves, and feedthroughs
  • System design — conductance, pump sizing, and pump-down behavior
  • Leak detection — helium mass-spectrometer leak hunting on real systems
  • Residual gas analysis and contamination diagnosis
  • Preventive maintenance, rebuild practice, and troubleshooting
  • Capstone — bring a vacuum system from atmosphere to base pressure and certify it leak-tight

Hazardous Materials & Waste Certification

2 weeks · ~16 hours · lecture + practical exercises

Safe handling, storage, and disposal of the chemicals and materials found in industrial and research settings. Practical, regulation-aware training that employers can rely on from day one.

Topics covered

  • Hazard communication — GHS labeling and reading Safety Data Sheets
  • Chemical storage, segregation, and compatibility
  • Personal protective equipment — selection, use, and limitations
  • Compressed gases and cryogens — cylinders, regulators, and safe handling
  • Heavy metals and toxic materials handling
  • Waste streams — identification, labeling, accumulation, and manifests (RCRA basics)
  • Spill prevention and response
  • Exposure monitoring and hygiene practices
  • Emergency procedures and incident reporting

Industrial Safety (OSHA) Certification

2 weeks · ~20 hours · aligned with OSHA outreach training

Workplace safety fundamentals for advanced manufacturing environments, aligned with OSHA general-industry outreach training. Graduates understand both their rights and the practices that keep a shop floor running without incident.

Topics covered

  • The OSHA framework — standards, inspections, and worker rights
  • Hazard identification and job-safety analysis
  • Hazard communication and chemical safety
  • Lockout/tagout — controlling hazardous energy
  • Electrical safety in industrial settings
  • Machine guarding and hand/power tool safety
  • Walking and working surfaces, ladders, and material handling
  • Fire prevention, egress, and emergency action plans
  • PPE and ergonomics
  • Incident investigation, recordkeeping, and reporting

Human Centered Design Certification

4 weeks · ~32 hours · workshop format with team project

A workshop-style program in design thinking: how to understand the people who will use a product, process, or piece of equipment, and design for them from the start. Valuable for technicians, engineers, and entrepreneurs alike.

Topics covered

  • The human-centered design process — empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test
  • User research — interviews, observation, and journey mapping
  • Defining the real problem — needs statements and requirements
  • Ideation techniques and concept selection
  • Rapid prototyping — from sketches to functional mockups
  • Usability evaluation and design iteration
  • Designing for safety, accessibility, and error prevention in equipment and processes
  • Communicating design decisions to stakeholders
  • Capstone — a team design project taken from user research to tested prototype
